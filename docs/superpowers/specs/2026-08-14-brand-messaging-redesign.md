# Yoonity Brand Messaging Redesign

## Goal

Revise the Yoonity Lab website messaging so industry–academia collaboration managers immediately understand what problems the lab solves, why it is credible, and how to request a collaboration meeting. Prospective graduate students and undergraduate researchers remain the secondary audience with a distinct path.

This specification defines brand context, audience priorities, positioning, messaging architecture, content boundaries, and the recommended sequence for later copy implementation. It does not implement or approve final page copy.

## Brand Foundation

Yoonity is an industry-problem-solving AI and information systems research lab at Dongguk University. It starts with complex organizational decision problems, designs AI- and data-based solutions, validates them in real settings, and explores the next transition toward Quantum-ready organizations.

The brand must feel professional, clear, practical, forward-looking, and collaborative. Its voice should preserve academic credibility without making readers decode academic jargon.

### Mission

Define real industrial and organizational problems as researchable questions, then develop them into verifiable solutions using technology and data.

### Core Values

- Start with the problem, not the technology.
- Validate ideas in real contexts.
- Preserve academic rigor.
- Create practical impact.
- Prepare organizations for the next technology transition.

## Audience Priority

### Primary Audience: Industry–Academia Collaboration Managers

The primary audience includes corporate, public-sector, and educational-organization managers responsible for industry–academia collaboration, AI strategy, digital transformation, or research programs.

They may have organizational problems and data but lack a partner who can define the research question, select an appropriate method, design a solution, and validate its effect. Their first questions are:

1. Can this lab understand our problem?
2. Has it handled comparable real-world work?
3. Can it go beyond analysis and validate a solution?
4. What collaboration formats are available?
5. Who should I contact, and how do I request a meeting?

The primary conversion is a collaboration email followed by a meeting request.

### Primary Personas

#### Corporate DX or AI Strategy Manager

This person is under pressure to begin an AI initiative but wants to clarify what should be tested before purchasing or building a solution. They care about business relevance, feasibility, expected deliverables, and a clear collaboration process more than publication volume alone.

#### Public-Sector Research or Policy Manager

This person needs a credible team to analyze field data, evaluate a policy or service, and produce a defensible model, report, or framework. They value methodological rigor, relevant project history, and the ability to work with institutional constraints.

### Secondary Audience: Research Applicants

The secondary audience comprises prospective graduate students and undergraduate researchers who want to turn real industry problems into projects, papers, patents, and other research outcomes. They need a distinct Join Yoonity path explaining research topics, participation, methods, growth opportunities, lab culture, and application steps.

### Excluded Audiences

The website should not optimize for organizations seeking only low-cost outsourced development, projects with no access to relevant data or field context, collaborations driven only by technology hype, or applicants interested only in credentials without concrete research interests.

## Positioning

### Category

- **Surface category**: AI and Quantum-AI research lab
- **Customer comparison set**: university labs, AI consultancies, data analytics firms, and solution developers
- **Opportunity category**: industry-problem-solving research partner

The opportunity category is preferred because “AI lab” alone does not make Yoonity meaningfully distinct. The selected category combines industry experience, research rigor, solution design, and validation.

### Competitive Map

Use these conceptual axes when evaluating copy and content:

- Technology-led to industry-problem-led
- Theory and analysis to design and field validation

Yoonity should occupy the industry-problem-led and design-and-field-validation territory. Traditional labs may lead with theory and publications, developers with implementation, and consultancies with strategy. Yoonity connects problem definition, research design, technology-enabled solutions, field validation, and academic extension.

### Positioning Territory

- **The space**: A research partner that begins with industrial decision problems instead of prescribing technology first.
- **The audience owned**: Organizations that want to apply and objectively validate AI, data, or future technologies in real work.
- **The moat**: The lab combines a professor's industry planning and data-science experience with information-systems research, industry projects, publications, patents, and student research outcomes. It can connect practical problem definition to design, validation, and scholarly output.

### Positioning Statements

**Formal**

For companies and institutions with complex organizational decision problems, Yoonity is the research partner that designs and validates AI- and data-based solutions in the field while preparing for a Quantum-ready transition.

**Public-facing headline**

> 복잡한 산업 문제를, 검증 가능한 해법으로.

**Public-facing support**

> 현장의 문제에서 출발해 AI와 데이터로 해결책을 설계하고 검증합니다. 그리고 다음 기술 전환까지 함께 준비합니다.

### Proof Points Available on the Current Website

- Projects involving LLM chatbots, customer-review analysis, recommendation algorithms, education innovation, policy, and shared-data use
- Collaboration experience with companies, government bodies, and educational institutions
- A principal investigator with both AI-service planning and data-science industry experience
- International and domestic publications and multiple academic awards
- Patents related to generative-AI systems and AI agents
- A research process that connects problem definition, design, field validation, and future readiness

Copy must not invent outcome metrics, customer quotations, or Quantum-AI achievements that the available source material does not substantiate.

### Positioning Boundaries

Yoonity must not present itself as a lab that can do every kind of AI work, overstate Quantum capability without evidence, force industry readers to interpret lists of papers and jargon, or resemble a general outsourced development vendor.

## Messaging Architecture

The website should answer the visitor's questions in this order:

1. **What does Yoonity solve?** Complex industrial decision problems.
2. **How does it work?** Problem definition, solution and research design, field validation, and expansion.
3. **What capabilities does it use?** AI and analytics, generative AI, and Quantum-AI or optimization when appropriate.
4. **Why should the visitor believe it?** Relevant projects, partners, publications, patents, awards, and industry experience.
5. **How can we collaborate?** Joint research, industry–academia projects, pilots, validation, analytics, and advisory work.
6. **How do we begin?** Send a collaboration inquiry and request a meeting.
7. **Where do applicants go?** A separate Join Yoonity section and recruitment path.

The core narrative is therefore:

> Industry problem → Yoonity method → appropriate capabilities → evidence → collaboration format → email and meeting request

## Page-Level Direction

### Home

Lead with the industry problem and outcome, not the technology portfolio. Introduce AI, generative AI, and Quantum-AI only after establishing relevance. Add visible proof and a direct collaboration action before the secondary recruitment route.

### About and Collaboration

Explain the lab's mission, collaboration formats, process, evidence, and a low-friction email or meeting request. Keep Join Yoonity visible but subordinate to the primary industry collaboration path.

### Research Areas

Describe each area through the organizational problem it addresses, the intervention or method, and the outcome that can be evaluated. Avoid presenting research areas as disconnected technology categories.

### Professor, Activities, Publications, and Researchers

Frame existing facts as evidence for the positioning without changing their substance. Add contextual introductions where lists currently require the reader to infer relevance.

## Recommended Work Sequence

1. Structure three to six representative collaboration cases as problem, approach, result, and partner. Unknown results must remain explicitly unknown until verified.
2. Use the `brand-messaging` skill to produce the headline, value proposition, message hierarchy, proof copy, audience-specific messages, and calls to action.
3. Use the `brand-voice` skill to define terminology, sentence length, Korean and English usage, jargon rules, and examples.
4. Audit every current page and classify copy as keep, revise, remove, or add.
5. Implement copy in this priority: Home, About and Collaboration, research areas, professor, researchers, and evidence pages.
6. Test the revised first screen with three to five collaboration managers and three to five prospective researchers. Ask what the lab does, whether it appears relevant and credible, and what action they would take next.

## Success Criteria

- A collaboration manager can describe Yoonity as an industry-problem-solving research partner after reading the first screen.
- The primary path to collaboration email and a meeting request is visible and unambiguous.
- AI, generative AI, and Quantum-ready work appear as capabilities within one problem-led method rather than three unrelated claims.
- Claims on the site are supported by current project, publication, patent, award, or career evidence.
- Prospective researchers can find a separate, clear recruitment path without competing with the primary collaboration message.
- The revised site can later track collaboration inquiries, meeting conversions, joint-project proposals, and applicant inquiries.

## Out of Scope

- Implementing the revised copy or page layout in this design phase
- Inventing project outcomes, testimonials, partner endorsements, or performance metrics
- Creating an automated meeting-booking system before the preferred operational process is specified
- Rebranding the visual identity or changing research facts
